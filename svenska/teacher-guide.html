Guide

How to run a shared Swedish dialog drill with a student.

About this site

This is a small practice tool focused on the most common Swedish words and connectives — prepositions (i / på / vid / hos), possessives (min/mitt/mina, sin/sitt/sina), question words (vem / var / vart / varifrån / vilken), time expressions (för 4 år sedan, i 2 år, sedan 2019, på halv 5), and short situational phrases (café, intro, work, housing, the doctor, the airport, etc.).

The intent is speed and accuracy on basics, not vocabulary expansion. Students who already understand a lot of Swedish but stumble on the connective grammar can use it to drill those atoms until they come out automatically.

The two pages

Database — phrase drill

Roughly 375 phrases across 21 categories (prepositions, possessives, modal verbs, common verbs with principal parts, café phrases, etc.). Click any card to reveal the English and Hungarian translation. Search box and category filter at the top. Intended for solo practice.

Dialog Session — generated dialogs

About 4,600 unique short dialogs (3–6 lines), generated from ~40 templates × typed vocabulary pools. Two speakers, A and B. Designed for a teacher and student to use together during a lesson — see below.

How a shared session works

A student opens the landing page, enters the session passcode, clicks Start session, and shares the resulting student link with the teacher (it's auto-copied to the clipboard). When the teacher opens that link, both browsers stay in sync: the teacher controls what gets revealed, and the student sees only what the teacher has revealed.

The two roles

Teacher Sees the entire dialog. Drives the session: presses Next, taps lines to reveal, toggles modes.
Student Sees only the lines the teacher has revealed. Their own lines may be visible or hidden depending on the active mode.

What the student sees in default mode

By default, the student sees their own lines (whichever speaker A or B is assigned to them) in plain English. The teacher's lines appear as placeholder bubbles: ···. The student produces the Swedish for their line out loud; the teacher then taps the line to reveal the Swedish translation so the student can verify.

Teacher controls

Tap any line Reveals both English and Swedish of that line — for both sides. Tap again to hide.
Reveal all / Hide all Flips every line at once. Useful for stepping through the full dialog after the student has attempted it.
Show all to student Master override — everything is visible to the student regardless of per-line state. Use for review / discussion.
Strict Hides the student's own lines too. The student sees only what the teacher reveals — including the lines they're meant to say. Harder mode; also works as listening practice.
Student speaks as: A / B Choose which speaker (A in blue or B in green) is the student. Default is B (the responder). Switch to A if the student should initiate.
Next dialog → Generates a new random dialog from one of ~40 templates. Both views jump together.
← Prev Returns to the previous dialog. Useful for review.

Recommended flow during a lesson

  1. The student opens the landing page, enters the passcode, clicks Start, and shares the student link with the teacher.
  2. The teacher opens the link. Both views connect — the top bar should show ● connected.
  3. A random dialog appears. The student's lines are coloured (green for B, blue for A); the teacher's lines appear as ··· placeholders on the student's screen.
  4. The teacher reads their line out loud, then taps it to reveal it on the student's screen.
  5. The student responds in Swedish (their next line). The teacher taps that line to reveal the model Swedish on both screens for comparison.
  6. When the dialog is finished or the student is stuck, the teacher taps Reveal all to discuss the full text.
  7. Tap Next dialog to continue. The goal is brisk pace.
Note: the system is designed for muscle-memory drilling, not realism. Some dialogs will be lightly absurd ("Want to try the coffee?", "We bought it last Friday"). That's intentional — the aim is for the student to produce the right preposition, pronoun, and verb form quickly and correctly, not to construct logically perfect conversations.

Errors

If the page displays an error code (Code-E01, etc.), the most common cause is a network blip; refreshing usually resolves it. Persistent errors should be reported to whoever maintains the site.
